Architecture, Datasets, and Linguistic Modeling

An overview of the machine learning design choices, training corpora, and interpretability frameworks underlying the VERIFAI platform.

1. Problem Formulation & Scope

Automated misinformation detection presents unique challenges due to stylistic evolution, weaponized sensationalism, and high-frequency content generation. Traditional black-box neural approaches frequently suffer from severe generalization degradation when exposed to unseen topics or emerging political events.

VERIFAI addresses this via an ensemble hybrid architecture that pairs transformer-based contextual representations with explicit stylometric indicators (such as hyperbole density, affective polarity, and named attribution verification), ensuring that predictions are both quantitatively robust and human-interpretable.

2. Training Corpora & Benchmarks

To mitigate single-dataset inductive bias, models are evaluated against three widely referenced academic benchmarks in natural language processing:

ISOT Fake News Dataset (University of Victoria)

44,898 Records

Contains real articles extracted from Reuters.com and unverified articles from flagged conspiracy blogs. Used primarily for evaluating stylistic wire datelines and sensational headline markers.

WELFake Unified Corpus (IEEE DataPort)

72,131 Records

A curated composite combining Kaggle Fake News, McIntire, and ISOT datasets to balance domain distribution across health, finance, foreign policy, and science reporting.

LIAR Benchmark (PolitiFact Corpus)

12,836 Records

Short-claim factual verification annotated across a 6-tier truthfulness scale (Pants-on-Fire, False, Barely-True, Half-True, Mostly-True, True). Used for calibrate borderline confidence levels.

3. Multi-Layer Feature Pipeline

Rather than relying exclusively on dense vector embeddings, the inference pipeline extracts four complementary linguistic feature dimensions:

  • A.
    Stylometric & Entropy Markers:

    Calculates Type-Token Ratio (TTR), uppercase letter percentage, exclamation density, and superlative/adverb ratios that correlate heavily with manufactured urgency.

  • B.
    Named Entity & Attribution Verification:

    Parses dependency grammar to detect reporting verbs ("confirmed", "stated", "published") paired with named institutions or recognized specialists versus anonymous claims ("insiders claim", "they don't want you to know").

  • C.
    Readability & Syntactic Complexity:

    Applies Flesch-Kincaid and Gunning-Fog algorithms to detect syntactic fragmentation commonly observed in autogenerated content farms.

4. Explainable AI (XAI) & Interpretability

A key design requirement for this final year project is transparency. In high-stakes media literacy and journalism, users must understand why a piece of content was flagged.

VERIFAI implements a token attribution mechanism inspired by LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) and Integrated Gradients. Each input token receives a calculated directional weight indicating whether it shifted the prediction toward Fabricated or Legitimate.

5. Ethical Boundaries & Limitations

Important Academic Disclaimer: Automated classification systems should serve as decision-support instruments rather than sole arbiters of truth. Edge cases such as political satire (e.g., The Onion), breaking unconfirmed breaking news, and nuanced editorial commentary require careful human fact-checking. VERIFAI includes user-feedback reporting mechanisms specifically to capture false positive edge cases for model retraining.
